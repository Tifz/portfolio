About

A data security and AI security governance practitioner with 8 years in security — formerly a Data Security Governance Lead at a major state-owned bank and a Senior Security Engineer on the vendor side. I specialize in migrating enterprise data security governance methodology into AI and Agent security: building two open-source AI security platforms and writing three long-form research articles covering industry trends, governance frameworks, and product practice.

Projects

1. AI Agent Release Governance Platform

React + TypeScriptFastAPIPostgreSQL + pgvectorDocker

A closed-loop release governance platform that answers "Is this agent ready to ship?" — linking agent profiles, knowledge bases, test matrices, risk reviews, evaluations, and release gates. Security coverage maps to OWASP LLM Top 10 (2026) and OWASP Agentic AI Top 10, including prompt injection, excessive agency, tool misuse, and MCP supply-chain risks.

Repository: github.com/Tifz/agent-release-governance

2. AI Product Security Risk Assessment Platform

Multi-AgentStreamlitISO/IEC 42001OWASP

A multi-agent pre-launch assessment tool for finance and government scenarios. Input an AI product's requirements and technical solution; automatically output a security risk map, a review question bank, and POC verification plans. Four industry templates (finance / government / internet / healthcare) map risks to 10+ laws and standards, including PIPL, MLPS 2.0, and ISO/IEC 42001.

Repository: github.com/Tifz/ai-product-security-risk-assessment

Articles

Full texts are in Chinese; English abstracts are provided below.

1. Data Security & AI Security: From Governance Foundations to the Paradigm Evolution in the Intelligent Era

A panoramic analysis of the two converging tracks and their international and domestic practices — Google, Anthropic, Microsoft, and CrowdStrike alongside ICBC, China Construction Bank, Tencent, and Huawei. It identifies five core trends: from compliance-driven to value-driven security, from LLM safety to Agent safety, and from siloed work to converged governance.

2. From Data Security Governance to AI Security Governance: Methodology Evolution and a Convergence Framework

Argues that the underlying logic of data security and AI security is the same. It summarizes the governance methodology practiced at a major state-owned bank, presents a four-in-one AI governance framework (organization, process, technology, operations), and lays out a unified convergence framework with industry-specific adaptation.

3. From Governance Methodology to Product Practice: Product Design Thinking for the Convergence of Data Security and AI Security

Documents the journey from research and methodology to product prototypes, including four design principles: a governance perspective, deterministic controls, shift-left coverage, and private deployment for highly regulated industries — exploring AI-driven data security and data security enabling AI security.
